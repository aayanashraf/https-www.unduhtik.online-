Why the video ID shows the date

TikTok video IDs are assigned in order over time. The first part of the number holds the creation time as Unix seconds (the count of seconds since 1 January 1970). This tool takes that part of the ID and converts it into a normal date. It does not contact TikTok to do this, so the check is quick and works even when you are offline after the link is pasted.

What the result tells you

The result shows the moment the video was created. You will see two times: your device time, which follows your time zone, and UTC, which is the same for everyone. The "How long ago" line counts from the current moment, so it changes each time you check.

The result does not show views, likes, the account owner's details, or any other data from the video. It only reads the date embedded in the ID.

When the date looks wrong

If a result says the ID does not produce a plausible date, check that you copied a full video ID or address and not a short link. If the date is in the future, the number is probably not a TikTok video ID. A video that was edited or uploaded again can also show an older date than the one you expect, because the ID reflects the original creation.

Privacy

The check runs in your browser. The date is calculated on your device from the ID you paste. Do not paste private information into the box, and remember that a link can still reveal a username because it is part of the address.
